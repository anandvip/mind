Silence

A 21-minute practice. Thoughts are not the problem; believing a thought is important, and following it, is what breaks the silence.

The method

1. Let thoughts pass, don't engageWhen a thought arrives, notice it, say a soft inner "not now", and return. Nothing to fix, nothing to solve.
2. Ask: to whom is this thought coming?From Ramana Maharshi's self-enquiry. The question turns attention back to the one who is aware, and the thought loses its grip.
3. Give the mind one small anchorFor the first three minutes, count breaths 1 to 10 and start again when you lose count. Or repeat So on the in-breath and Ham on the out-breath. Drop the anchor once the mind settles.
4. Rest in the gapThere is a natural pause after each out-breath. Rest attention there. That pause is a small taste of silence, and it widens.
5. Don't grade the sessionA noisy sit where you came back fifty times trains the mind more than an easy one. Coming back is the practice.
6. Keep the body still, the eyes softA still body quietens the mind. Small fidgets feed it.

Opening ritual (one minute)

  1. Light a diya or incense, or simply wash your hands and face.
  2. Chant Om three times, long and slow. Feel the hum in the chest.
  3. Set the intention: "For 21 minutes, nothing to become, nothing to fix."
  4. At the end, sit one more minute doing nothing, then bow.

The way back (when you notice you've drifted)

  1. Stop.
  2. Three slow breaths.
  3. Om, three times.
  4. Reread today's one-line goal.
  5. Resume at the next slot. No self-blame.

Times traditionally favourable for silence

  • Brahma muhurat, roughly 90 to 40 minutes before sunrise: the mind is naturally quiet before the day's input.
  • Sandhya, the junctions about ten minutes either side of sunrise and sunset, when day and night meet.
  • Inward-turning nakshatras: Mula, Ashlesha, Jyeshtha, Revati, Shatabhisha; and the Moon in Karka, Vrishchika or Meena.
  • Amavasya and Ekadashi for inward practice; Purnima for devotion.
  • Difficult Moon days (Chandrashtama) are not a bad time to sit. Turning inward is traditionally the best use of them.
Timing is traditional guidance. The effect comes from showing up daily, not from the slot itself.

Why this page exists. In daily practice the meditator noticed a weak link: during the 21-minute sits, the mind's race begins the moment a thought is taken as important, and identifying with it hijacks the session and defeats the purpose of experiencing silence. A second weak link showed up in daily life: after an immersive stretch of flow, the ladder back to practice is habitually forgotten, along with the reason for doing it. This page gathers the method, the opening ritual and the way back in one place, so the reason and the return are always one click away.

Drawn from traditional teachings (Ramana Maharshi's self-enquiry, So-Ham breath practice, classical Jyotish timing). Traditional guidance, not certainty.
